## Research Findings

This protocol pins every formula, threshold, and dataset choice the artifact plan called for.

**Part A — Discriminative n-grams and labeling cost.** The MI formula to use is the classical discrete/expected mutual information computed over the 2x2 contingency table of (n-gram present/absent) x (binary label), estimated from pilot-set frequencies with a small Laplace-style smoothing constant to avoid log(0) cells [3,4]. This is distinct from, and preferred over, scikit-learn's `mutual_info_classif`, whose documented estimator uses k-nearest-neighbor distances designed for continuous or mixed features [4] — since n-gram presence/absence is already binary, the closed-form contingency-table MI is the correct, simpler tool, and using `mutual_info_classif` directly would silently apply the wrong estimator. Yang & Pedersen's 1997 comparative study is the canonical reference for MI as one of several text-categorization feature-selection criteria, and explicitly documents MI's known bias toward rare terms as a weakness [3] — motivating the df>=2 pilot cutoff (a convention with no single canonical numeric source, but consistent with the general low-document-frequency caution in that literature). Recommended pilot sizes are 100/200/400 labeled examples, and every result table must report `total_labeling_cost = pilot_size` as an explicit line item — the method is never 'label-free' when a labeled pilot underlies its vocabulary. The recommended top-k rule is a permutation-null construction: shuffle pilot labels B=200 times, recompute MI under the null, and use the 95th percentile of the per-shuffle MAXIMUM MI (a family-wise-error control, more conservative and more defensible for a large vocabulary than a per-comparison null) as the significance cutoff. The MI-free robustness alternative is `|P(t|pos)-P(t|neg)|` ranked descending with the identical top-k scheme, compared to the MI-based set via Jaccard overlap of the top-k sets and Spearman correlation of resulting n_hat* across the domain matrix — a required ablation, not optional, since a positive result specific to one arbitrary feature-selection formula is much weaker evidence than one robust to the exact selection rule.

**Part B — Coverage curve.** Good-Turing's missing-mass estimator is `p0_hat = N1/N` [5,6], adapted here to a document-occurrence-count unit (a convention choice, since the classical NLP/ecology literature more often uses raw token/individual counts [5,6], but document-presence is what TF-IDF's document-frequency weighting actually consumes) giving `C(n) = 1 - f1(n)/N(n)` per unlabeled-pool subsample of size n. Chao1 serves as a secondary cross-check, using the bias-corrected form `S_obs + f1(f1-1)/(2(f2+1))` uniformly (not just when f2=0) to avoid a discontinuity, per scikit-bio's default behavior and the general ecology literature [7,8,9]. A mandatory instability flag marks any (dataset, n, draw) with `f2<10` as UNSTABLE (a stated, not universally canonical, ecology rule-of-thumb [7,8]), with a required per-dataset table of the unstable-point fraction; grid points where more than half of B=100 draws are unstable are excluded from curve-fitting. The subsampling procedure draws B=100 samples without replacement per grid point `{50,100,200,400,800,1600,3200}` (capped at pool size), reporting the median C(n) and a bootstrap 95% CI (2.5/97.5 percentiles) rather than a point estimate. The discriminative vocabulary is fixed once from the labeled pilot and never recomputed per unlabeled subsample, to avoid confounding the coverage curve with a moving target (and because the unlabeled pool, by construction, has no labels to recompute MI from).

**Part C — Crossover fitting and cross-domain protocol.** Empirical crossover n* is defined as the first grid point in `{50,100,200,500,1000,1500,2000}` where the DistilBERT-minus-TF-IDF accuracy gap exceeds the pooled standard error of the two means (>=5 seeds each) — removing ambiguity about noise-driven crossings. The label-free predictor is `n_hat* = min{n : 1-C(n) < tau}`, with tau — the method's single free parameter — fit only from the calibration dataset's own `(1-C(n*))` value and reported explicitly in every table. Three domains were verified directly from HuggingFace dataset cards: Rotten Tomatoes (10,662 short movie-review snippets, binary, license 'unknown' on its card) [10]; SST-2/GLUE (67,349 train examples per common citation, HF parquet files sized 3.11 MB train / 72.8 kB val / 148 kB test, license 'other') [11] — explicitly flagged as a WEAK near-transfer pair against Rotten Tomatoes since both are short, curated movie-review sentiment corpora; and cardiffnlp/tweet_eval's sentiment subset (tens of thousands of rows, 3-class, register-shifted social-media text, license 'unknown' plus inherited Twitter ToS) [12] as the mandatory genuinely-distinct third domain, with fancyzhx/yelp_polarity (560k/38k train/test, binary, business-review register) [13] documented as a fallback if tweet_eval's 3-class labels prove disruptive to binarize. The full protocol is a 3x2=6 directed calibrate/predict matrix; this research artifact's own $10/3h budget applies only to its own web-research calls, so no matrix subsetting is required at this stage, though the eventual experiment executor is instructed to preserve at least one far-transfer pair over any second near-transfer pair if its own compute budget forces a cut. Success is scored per pair as falling within the 0.5x-2x band around true n*, plus a per-dataset bootstrap-CI'd Spearman rho between the coverage-curve shape and the accuracy gap across the shared n grid.

**Part D — Failure modes and disconfirmation triggers.** Five failure modes are documented with mechanism: (i) the f2<10 small-doubleton regime, which directly undermines both Good-Turing and Chao1 since both estimators are built from the rarest-frequency classes [5,7,8]; (ii) pilot-noise-driven vocabulary variance, distinct from and potentially confounded with genuine coverage-mechanism variance; (iii) near-transfer domain contamination between RT and SST-2, motivating the mandatory distinct third domain; (iv) tau's single-degree-of-freedom fragility, with a stated robustness check comparing single-dataset tau against a multi-dataset-mean tau when >=3 datasets are available; (v) a monotonicity precondition on 1-C(n) that must be visually confirmed before fitting, since vocabulary drift in a mixed-genre unlabeled pool could make the curve non-monotonic and the crossover rule ill-defined. Disconfirmation triggers restate the hypothesis's own success criteria (order-of-magnitude n_hat* misses, or a flat/uncorrelated coverage-vs-accuracy-gap relationship) and add one new trigger from this protocol: if more than half of all grid points across datasets fall in the f2<10 unstable regime, the entire experiment should be reported as NOT INTERPRETABLE rather than scored pass/fail, since a coverage estimate built on an admittedly unreliable measurement layer carries no evidential weight in either direction.

**Confidence and remaining uncertainty.** The MI/Chao1/Good-Turing formulas themselves are drawn from primary or near-primary sources and are high-confidence. The exact numeric conventions flagged as 'chosen here, no canonical source' (df>=2 cutoff, Laplace smoothing constant, f2<10 threshold, document-occurrence-count units for Good-Turing) are reasonable, literature-consistent choices but are genuinely not backed by a single canonical citation, and the report says so rather than presenting them as settled. Dataset row counts for tweet_eval's sentiment subset were read from the aggregate HF card's subset listing rather than a subset-specific card page, so the exact split sizes should be re-verified against the `sentiment` config specifically when the experiment is implemented.
